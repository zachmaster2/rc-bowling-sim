# RC Bowling Simulator

A phone web page for trying out the controls of a remote-controlled bowling ball: a first-person view of a regulation lane on the top of the screen and two virtual thumb sticks on the bottom.

**Open it on a phone, held upright:** https://zachmaster2.github.io/rc-bowling-sim/

## How to drive

1. **Two sticks** (hold the phone in both hands). Put your left thumb anywhere on the left of the bottom panel and push up: that is the **Main stick**. Its centre is exactly where your thumb lands, so a press by itself does nothing; its ring may run past the panel's edge. The ring stays where your thumb landed for as long as you hold it, however far your thumb goes: past the ring's edge the knob stops on the ring and the stick reads the direction from the ring's centre to your thumb (so to turn the ball round, bring your thumb back past the centre). The ball travels in the direction the stick points, straight up being straight down the lane, and how far you push sets how hard the ball pushes. The Main stick is one for one: the Hold trigger, below, is what holds a line. Your right thumb, anywhere on the right of the panel, works the **Precision stick**: a second stick of the same size, of which only left–right is read. Push it left or right for small aims (up to 5° by default); let go and the aim springs back to straight. While that thumb is down, the Main stick is held as it is: see the Hold trigger, below.
2. You stand at the back of the approach and walk up behind the ball as far as the foul line. **Walk up with the ball** has two ways: **+ zoom** (the default) also closes the view in on the ball and the pins as the ball goes; **Walk only** keeps the view's zoom and tilt from the start, so walking up shows as walking.
3. After each ball a card shows where it hit, the entry angle, the speed, and what published USBC data says a hit like that strikes.
4. **Reset** puts the same ball back at its start at any time, the moment it is touched, and takes where the phone (or the controller) points at that moment as straight down the lane. There is no Recenter button: every new ball recentres. A ball put at its start waits for a fresh press of the stick: if your thumb was already holding the stick (at Reset, Ball 2 or New frame), that stick does nothing, however you drag it, until you let go and press again. Reset, the Lock toggle button and the result card's button each answer their own touch, so a second finger works them while a thumb is down on a stick: the Lock toggle button and Reset act the moment they are touched; the card's button acts when the finger lifts on it (slide off first to cancel).

The gear button opens the settings: the Precision stick's range, sensing errors, the start board and distance, walking up, the two arrows and the shot log. There is one mode: the Lock toggle button, below, is the only switch.

## Two sticks, the Hold trigger and the Lock toggle button

- **Precision stick range** sets how far full left or right on the Precision stick moves the aim (±5° by default).
- **The Hold trigger** holds the Main stick as it is. While the Main stick is pushing, squeeze the Hold trigger and keep it squeezed: the stick's angle and strength are held, and you can take your thumb off it (or move it by accident) without the ball noticing. Everything else still works: the Precision stick makes its small aims, turning the controller still steers (with the Lock toggle button off), and the Stop button still brakes while it is pressed - let it go and the held push is back, so short presses trim the speed. Let the Hold trigger go and the Main stick has the ball again (with no thumb on it, the push ends). Squeezed with nothing being pushed, it holds nothing. Reset and a new ball end it. **On a phone the Hold trigger is your right thumb: while it is down on the Precision stick, the Main stick is held** - push with the left thumb, put the right thumb down, lift the left, aim with the right, and lift the right to let go. The held stick stays drawn in amber where it was taken. On a game controller it is the left trigger.
- **The Lock toggle button** (LOCK, between the sticks) is the one switch between the two ways of aiming. **On** - as the page opens, the button lit: only the sticks steer, and turning the phone or the controller does nothing. **Off**: turning the phone (or the RC Bowling controller) turns the pointing direction too, and the sticks work on top of that; the top bar shows the aim and the amber pointing marker shows where the phone points. Switched on again, it freezes the pointing where it then is. It stays as you set it - a new ball does not change it - and it acts the moment it is touched, with or without a thumb on a stick. **Every new ball starts pointing straight down the lane:** Reset, Ball 2 and New frame take where the phone points at that moment as straight, and set a frozen pointing straight too. The first time you switch it off, an iPhone asks for motion access (if nothing appears, tap the small "Tap to enable motion" button under Reset). Without motion sensors (a desktop browser, or access refused) a drag strip above the sticks and the arrow keys turn the controller instead.

## How the ball answers

- **Point and push:** the ball travels where the controller points, turned by the Main stick's angle; how far the stick is pushed sets how hard the ball pushes.
- **Changing direction:** a push ahead of or beside the way the ball is rolling turns the ball onto your push at full thrust, and it keeps its speed; a push back against its roll slows it on its own line, and then it goes where you push (between 90° and 110° from its roll the one runs smoothly into the other).
- **Course arrow** (on by default): the blue arrow at the foot of the lane view shows where the ball is being sent right now: where the controller points (the frozen aim while the Lock toggle button is on), turned by the Main stick (the held stick while the Hold trigger is squeezed), plus the Precision stick. The amber **pointing marker** beside it (with the Lock toggle button off) shows where the phone points.

Settled on 17 and 18 November 2026 and not settings: two sticks, both regular sticks of the same size; the direct change of direction; the stick read from its ring's centre; the Precision stick as an offset that springs back; one mode, with the Lock toggle button as its switch; every new ball recentring. What was tried before and removed is no longer in the page at all: the one-stick controls (the stick curve, the Hold button), the second scheme (hold the line), the main stick's centre band, the precision stick as a slider, Nudge, the earlier law for changing direction, the "Turn the controller with the phone" setting and the Recenter button. A phone that had any of those stored simply drops them.

## Driving it with the controller, on a TV

The simulator also reads a Bluetooth gamepad through the browser's Gamepad API, so that a hand-held controller can drive it on a big screen: a laptop running the page in Chrome, shown on a TV over HDMI. An iPhone cannot host such a controller (iOS accepts only console and MFi gamepads, and Safari has no Web Bluetooth).

1. Pair the controller with the computer (Windows: Settings > Bluetooth & devices > Add device; it appears as "RC Bowling Controller"), open the page in Chrome, and press any button on it; Chrome shows a gamepad to a page only after a button press.
2. While a controller is connected the touch panel is hidden, the lane view fills the screen, the text is sized for a TV, and its sticks and its Stop, Lock and Hold keys are drawn in the lower left corner.
3. The Main stick drives exactly as the touch stick does. With the Lock toggle button off, the controller's own turn (its gyro) sets the pointing direction, as the phone's does.
4. **The Stop button** brakes the ball while it is held, as hard as the lane's grip allows; on the result card it takes the next ball. **The Lock toggle button** switches between the sticks alone and turning the controller to aim as well; the **Hold trigger**, while held, holds the Main stick (above). **Reset** puts the ball back and takes where the controller points as straight ahead.
5. A standard game controller (Xbox, PlayStation) drives with its left stick and makes small aims with its right stick. Its left trigger is the Hold trigger and its A (or cross) button is the Stop button (Setup can move both). It cannot sense turning: with the Lock toggle button off, the arrow keys turn the pointing direction instead.
6. **Settings > Controller** shows what the page sees of it: its axes and the buttons being pressed. **Setup** sets which axis and button does what, so a different gamepad works too.

The stage-1 controller is a design of its own: a printed two-handed shell laid out like an Xbox 360 controller, an ESP32-S3 board, an IMU, two thumb-sticks and four buttons, no soldering. Its firmware reports the Main stick on axes 1 and 2, the turn on axis 3 (heading / 180°, positive to the right) and the Precision stick's left–right on axis 4, and the Stop button, the Lock toggle button, a key the page no longer reads (it was Recenter) and Reset on buttons 1 to 4. The sticks only move: pressing one down is not reported. These are the page's defaults.

## Notes

- The lane, its markings and the pins are drawn to regulation dimensions, seen from the eye height of a 5 ft 9 in driver standing behind the approach.
- The view always holds the ball, the lane's width at the ball and the pins, framed as tightly as that allows and never larger than life (life size assumes the phone about 35 cm from your eyes). The pins therefore grow as the ball moves away from you. The ball starts 1.5 m before the foul line, 3 m ahead of you; **Ball start** in the settings moves it.
- The pins fall by rigid-body physics that is not validated for pin carry. The percentage on the result card comes from a published USBC chart (Stremmel, "Entry Angle", Fig. 1) and is the better guide.
- The page opens with the Lock toggle button on (sticks only). With it off, the phone's motion sensors turn the controller: they need a secure (https) page and, on an iPhone, your leave, asked for the first time you switch it off; without them (a desktop browser, for instance) a drag strip above the sticks and the arrow keys turn the controller.
- **The shot log** (Settings > Copy shot log) keeps the last 60 balls. Each holds the settings it was driven with (`set`), one row every 0.1 s (`series`; `cols` names the columns: `t, x, y, vx, vy, psi_c, stick_deg, stick_defl, eye_s, fine_deg, locked, held` - the stick columns are the raw Main stick, whatever the Hold trigger holds) and the intervals of the Lock toggle button, the Hold trigger and the Stop button (`lock`, `trig`, `stop`). Logs made before the evening of 17 November 2026 also carry the removed options' columns (`reaim_deg`, `line_psi0`) and settings; logs made before 18 November carry `phoneTurn` and `padTurn` in `set`, where newer ones carry `turn` (what turns the controller: `phone`, `strip` or `controller`).
- It is one file, `index.html`, loading three.js r128 and cannon.js 0.6.2 from cdnjs.
