# RC Bowling Simulator

A phone web page for trying out the controls of a remote-controlled bowling ball: a first-person view of a regulation lane on the top of the screen and a virtual thumb stick on the bottom.

**Open it on a phone, held upright:** https://zachmaster2.github.io/rc-bowling-sim/

## How to drive

1. **Two sticks** (the default; hold the phone in both hands). Put your left thumb anywhere on the left of the bottom panel and push up: that is the main stick. Its centre is exactly where your thumb lands, so a press by itself does nothing; its ring may run past the panel's edge. The ring stays where your thumb landed for as long as you hold it, however far your thumb goes: past the ring's edge the knob stops on the ring and the stick reads the direction from the ring's centre to your thumb (so to turn the ball round, bring your thumb back past the centre). **Pulling back: At once** in the settings is another way to try: the ring still stays put, but past its edge your thumb slips over the knob as on a real stick at its stop, so from wherever your thumb is, pulling back turns the stick round straight away (one ring radius back is centred, one ring width back is full the other way); the knob is then no longer under your thumb after a long push. The ball travels in the direction the stick points, straight up being straight down the lane, and how far you push sets how hard the ball pushes. Within 10° of straight up the main stick drives exactly straight, so a wobbling thumb does not steer. Your right thumb, anywhere on the right of the panel, works the **precision stick**: slide it left or right for small aims (up to 5° by default).
2. You stand at the back of the approach and walk up behind the ball as far as the foul line. **Walk up with the ball** has two ways: **+ zoom** (the default) also closes the view in on the ball and the pins as the ball goes; **Walk only** keeps the view's zoom and tilt from the start, so walking up shows as walking.
3. After each ball a card shows where it hit, the entry angle, the speed, and what published USBC data says a hit like that strikes.
4. **Reset** puts the same ball back at its start at any time.

The gear button opens the settings: control scheme, one or two sticks and how they respond, sensing errors, the start board and distance, walking up, the shot log, and **Turn the controller with the phone**. With that on, turning the phone turns the controller's pointing direction and **Recenter** sets "straight down the lane" to where the phone points now (allow motion access when asked).

## Two sticks, the trigger and LOCK

- **Main stick centre** (±10° by default): within this angle of straight up the main stick drives straight; from twice the angle it is one for one, with a smooth blend between. 0 makes it one for one everywhere.
- **Precision stick:** **Offset** (the default) aims while you hold it and springs back to straight when you let go; **Nudge** moves the aim while you hold it and the aim stays where you leave it (each new ball starts straight again). **Precision range** sets how far (Offset, ±5° by default) or how fast (Nudge, 5° a second) full left or right moves the aim.
- **The trigger** holds the main stick as it is. While the main stick is pushing, squeeze the trigger and keep it squeezed: the stick's angle and strength are held, and you can take your thumb off it (or move it by accident) without the ball noticing. Everything else still works: the precision stick makes its small aims, turning the controller still steers, and STOP still brakes while it is pressed - let STOP go and the held push is back, so short presses of STOP trim the speed. Let the trigger go and the main stick has the ball again (with no thumb on it, the push ends). Squeezed with nothing being pushed, it holds nothing. Reset and a new ball end it. **On a phone the trigger is your right thumb: while it is down on the precision stick, the main stick is held** - push with the left thumb, put the right thumb down, lift the left, aim with the right, and lift the right to let go. The held stick stays drawn in amber where it was taken. On a game controller it is the left trigger (and the right trigger is STOP).
- **LOCK** (between the sticks) appears whenever the pointing direction can turn: with **Turn the controller with the phone** on, or with the RC Bowling controller connected. Press it and the pointing direction is frozen where it was: turning no longer steers, only the sticks do. Press it again to steer by pointing again. Every new ball, and Reset, starts unlocked, so the first push always goes where you point. While it is on, the button is lit and the top bar says LOCKED; the blue course arrow stays put while the amber pointing marker follows the phone.
- **Sticks: One** brings back the earlier controls for comparison: one stick with its **Stick curve**, and the **Hold button**. The shot log records which controls each ball was driven with.

## The two control schemes

- **A, point and push:** the ball travels where the controller points, turned by the stick's angle. **Stick curve** (one stick; 50 % by default) softens the stick within 30° of straight ahead: 0 % not at all (one for one), 100 % most (designer 25's curve); 10° of stick gives 6.3° of course at 50 %, 2.6° at 100 %.
- **Changing direction** (both schemes): **Direct** (the default) or **As before**. With *Direct*, a push ahead of or beside the way the ball is rolling turns the ball onto your push at full thrust, and it keeps its speed; a push back against its roll slows it on its own line, and then it goes where you push (between 90° and 110° from its roll the one runs smoothly into the other). With *As before* the ball first cancels its speed across your push: a push to the side brakes it almost to rest before it moves over, and a push back and to one side swings it out to the other side first. For the small corrections of a normal shot the two steer the same. The shot log records which one each ball was driven with.
- **B, hold the line:** the push from rest takes the line where the controller points; after that the ball holds that line. Lean the stick and the ball moves over, and the line keeps part of each lean. **Lean slope** sets how much course a degree of lean gives (between 10° and 45° of lean); **Line keeps a lean** sets how long a lean must be held for the line to keep all of it (*never* keeps nothing). Turning the controller after the push does nothing within 15°.
- **Hold button** (one stick; off by default; choosing a side turns phone turning on, which it needs): a button for the other thumb, at the left or right of the lane view. Under A, holding it locks where the controller points and letting go jumps back to where the phone points. Under B, holding it lets the phone swing the line and letting go leaves the line where it is.
- **Course arrow** (on by default): the blue arrow at the foot of the lane view shows where the ball is being sent right now: under A the aim (HOLD's or LOCK's frozen aim while it is on) turned by the stick through the curve, or with two sticks through the main stick's centre band plus the precision stick; under B the held line with its lean. The amber **pointing marker** beside it (with phone turning on) shows where the phone points, so HOLD and B's held line show as the two arrows parting.

## Driving it with the controller, on a TV

The simulator also reads a Bluetooth gamepad through the browser's Gamepad API, so that a hand-held controller can drive it on a big screen: a laptop running the page in Chrome, shown on a TV over HDMI. An iPhone cannot host such a controller (iOS accepts only console and MFi gamepads, and Safari has no Web Bluetooth).

1. Pair the controller with the computer (Windows: Settings > Bluetooth & devices > Add device; it appears as "RC Bowling Controller"), open the page in Chrome, and press any button on it; Chrome shows a gamepad to a page only after a button press.
2. While a controller is connected the touch panel is hidden, the lane view fills the screen, the text is sized for a TV, and its sticks and its STOP and LOCK (or HOLD) keys are drawn in the lower left corner.
3. The stick drives exactly as the touch stick does. The controller's own turn (its gyro) sets the pointing direction, as the phone does with phone turning on.
4. **STOP** brakes the ball while it is held, as hard as the lane's grip allows; on the result card it takes the next ball. **HOLD** means what the on-screen Hold button means (one stick); with two sticks the same key is **LOCK**. **Recenter** sets straight ahead to where the controller points now, and **Reset** puts the ball back.
5. With **Sticks: Two**, a standard game controller (Xbox, PlayStation) drives with its left stick and makes small aims with its right stick. Its left trigger is the trigger and its right trigger is STOP (Setup can move both). It cannot sense turning, so LOCK does not appear with it.
6. **Settings > Controller** shows what the page sees of it: its axes and the buttons being pressed. **Setup** sets which axis and button does what, so a different gamepad works too.

The stage-1 controller is a design of its own: a printed two-handed shell laid out like an Xbox 360 controller, an ESP32-S3 board, an IMU, two thumb-sticks and four buttons, no soldering. Its firmware (2.3) reports the left stick on axes 1 and 2, the turn on axis 3 (heading / 180°, positive to the right) and the right stick's left–right on axis 4, and STOP, LOCK, Recenter and Reset on buttons 1 to 4. The sticks only move: pressing one down is not reported. These are the page's defaults.

## Notes

- The lane, its markings and the pins are drawn to regulation dimensions, seen from the eye height of a 5 ft 9 in driver standing behind the approach.
- The view always holds the ball, the lane's width at the ball and the pins, framed as tightly as that allows and never larger than life (life size assumes the phone about 35 cm from your eyes). The pins therefore grow as the ball moves away from you. The ball starts 1.5 m before the foul line, 3 m ahead of you; **Ball start** in the settings moves it.
- The pins fall by rigid-body physics that is not validated for pin carry. The percentage on the result card comes from a published USBC chart (Stremmel, "Entry Angle", Fig. 1) and is the better guide.
- Turning with the phone is off by default. When it is on, motion sensors need a secure (https) page and, on an iPhone, a tap to allow them; without them (a desktop browser, for instance) a drag strip above the stick and the arrow keys turn the controller.
- It is one file, `index.html`, loading three.js r128 and cannon.js 0.6.2 from cdnjs.
